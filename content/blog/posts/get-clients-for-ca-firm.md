Most Indian families have a doctor they never chose. Your father went to him, so you went to him, and when you moved cities you asked a relative for the name of someone like him. Nobody shopped. Nobody compared. The family doctor's practice grew at exactly the rate that families in the neighbourhood grew, and not one patient faster.

Then there is the cardiologist across town. Nobody inherits a cardiologist. People seek her out, by name, because a family doctor sent them, because they read something she wrote, or because she is the person the whole city knows for one thing. She is not allowed to advertise any more than the family doctor is. She gets found anyway.

Most CA practices in India are run like the family doctor. The clients came with a parent's practice, or with the first employer's network, or through a relative who needed returns filed. The work is good, the clients stay, and the practice grows at the rate that its clients' friends happen to need a CA. When someone asks how to get clients for a CA firm, they are usually asking how to stop waiting for that.

This article is my answer. It sits under my wider guide to [marketing for professional services firms](/marketing-for-professional-services), which covers why expert work is so hard to buy. Here I take the particular case of the chartered accountant: why practices stay referral-bound, what the profession's rules actually close off, why compliance work ends in a fee haggle, and how a CA firm gets found by the clients it wants while the partners are buried in returns.

## Why CA Practices Stay Referral-Bound

Three things keep a CA practice on referrals, and only one of them is the rulebook.

The first is how the work is bought. Nobody wakes up wanting an accountant. A business owner needs a GST registration, a tax audit, a set of books a bank will accept, and they ask the nearest trusted person for a name. The CA is chosen at the moment of need, from whoever is already known. A practice that is not already known at that moment does not exist.

The second is what the practice sells. Ask most firms what they do and you will get the same board as on the next twenty doors: audit, taxation, GST, company law, accounting, advisory. That list describes the profession, not the firm. A buyer who cannot tell two practices apart chooses the one a friend uses, which is another way of saying referrals.

The third is the rules, and the rules are the reason most CAs give. They are also the most misunderstood of the three, so they get their own section.

## What the ICAI Rules Actually Close Off

The Chartered Accountants Act and the ICAI Code of Ethics treat it as professional misconduct for a CA in practice to solicit clients or work, directly or indirectly, whether by circular, advertisement, personal communication or interview. Advertising is permitted only within the narrow limits the Council sets out in its guidelines. I am a marketing consultant, not your ethics committee, so read the current Code and guidelines yourself before you publish anything promotional, and ask the Institute if you are unsure.

Look at what the rules close off, though. They close off asking for work. Cold emails to businesses that never asked to hear from you. Calls to a list of companies. Ads promising the lowest audit fee in the city. In other words, they close off exactly the tactics that work worst for a trust purchase anyway. I would not send a CA firm near cold outreach even if the rules allowed it, for the same reason I kept a boutique law firm away from it: nobody hands their books to a stranger who interrupted them.

What the rules leave open is being found. Sharing what you know. Writing about a change in the law and what it means for a particular kind of business. Speaking at an industry association. Having a website a buyer can read when they look you up. Being the name that comes to mind because the buyer has been reading you for a year. That is how the cardiologist got known, and it is how the CA firms that grow without waiting get known. The rules do not stop a CA from marketing. They stop a CA from marketing badly.

## Why Compliance Work Ends in a Fee Haggle

![Identical compliance services compared only on fee](/images/blog/get-clients-for-ca-firm/inline-1.webp)

Most CA firms that do try to grow run into the same wall: the new clients they win are price shoppers, and the old clients start asking why the fee went up.

That is not bad luck. Compliance work is the most comparable thing a CA firm sells. A GST return filed by one firm looks exactly like a GST return filed by another. An income tax return is either filed on time or it is not. The buyer cannot see the judgement that went into it, so they compare the one thing they can see: the fee. And every year the floor drops, as online filing services and accounting software pull the simplest work towards a few hundred rupees a return.

A firm that markets compliance has volunteered for that comparison. Its website lists the returns it files, its proposals quote per filing, and the buyer lines the quote up against three others and against the software. The partners feel this as a sales problem: clients who negotiate every rupee and leave for someone cheaper. It is a marketing problem that shows up at the sales stage. The firm told the market it sells something interchangeable, and the market believed it.

Compliance still matters. It is the reason a client meets you, and done well it is the reason they stay. It is just a poor thing to be known for.

## Positioning Past "Audit, Tax and Compliance"

The way out is the cardiologist's way: be known for one kind of client and one kind of problem. The general method is in [positioning for consulting firms](/blog/positioning-for-consulting-firms). For a CA firm, the choice usually comes from one of three directions.

**A kind of client.** Exporters. D2C brands selling on marketplaces. Funded startups. Doctors and clinic chains. NRIs with property and income in India. Family businesses moving to the second generation. Each has its own tax questions, its own regulators and its own vocabulary, and each would rather have a CA who already understands their world than one who has to learn it on their fee. A firm that is visibly for exporters will be recommended by exporters to other exporters, which is a referral network that works on purpose.

**A kind of problem.** Getting a startup ready for due diligence before a fundraise. Untangling a GST mess after a year of growth. Restructuring a family business for succession. Problems like these arrive with urgency and a budget, and the buyer searches for the problem, not for "chartered accountant near me".

**A kind of engagement.** Some firms position by how they work rather than whom they serve. Virtual CFO services for companies too small for a full-time finance head is the clearest example in India, and it is a position because it describes a relationship the buyer can picture, not a list of returns.

The fear is always the same: if we say we are for exporters, we will lose the general work. In practice, nobody leaves a CA who does their returns well because the CA also writes about export incentives. What changes is that new clients start arriving for a reason other than proximity.

## Packaging Advisory Beyond Compliance

![A practice that grows from compliance into advisory](/images/blog/get-clients-for-ca-firm/inline-2.webp)

Position chosen, the next question is what a new client can actually buy. "Advisory services" on a website is not an answer. Nobody knows what it means, what it costs or when it ends, so nobody asks for it.

The fix is to package the thinking you already do into something with a name, a scope and an end. [How to productise a service](/blog/how-to-productize-your-service) covers the general approach. For a CA firm it looks like this:

- **Name it for the outcome.** "Due diligence readiness for funded startups" invites a conversation. "Advisory" invites a quote.
- **Give it a shape.** Four weeks, a review of the books and contracts, a list of what an investor will flag, a fixed scope of fixes, a short report the founder can show the board.
- **Make the first step small.** A one-time review of a company's tax position, or a GST health check, is easy for a stranger to approve. It shows them how you think, and it tells you whether there is a longer relationship to be had.
- **Let compliance sit inside it.** The returns become part of the retainer that follows, not the thing the client bought you for.

This is what changed the boutique law firm I worked with. It had sold time, billed by the hour when something went wrong, and every new matter arrived by referral. We packaged its thinking into a defined advisory engagement a stranger could understand and approve before any crisis. The partners never learned to sell. They did not need to, because describing a defined service is something a professional can do all day without discomfort. The story is in [a law firm that could finally be bought, not just referred](/case-studies/cs-05-boutique-law-firm), and nearly every line of it applies to a CA practice.

Packaging also moves the fee conversation. A defined engagement aimed at a specific outcome is priced for the outcome, and there is nothing in a competitor's per-filing quote to line it up against. [Value-based pricing for consultants](/blog/value-based-pricing-for-consultants) goes into how to set that number.

## Getting Found Without Asking for Work

![Other advisors around the client, with the firm at the table](/images/blog/get-clients-for-ca-firm/inline-3.webp)

This is the part the rules shape most, and it is the part where CA firms have the biggest advantage, because almost none of them do it well.

**Write about the problems you have chosen.** Every Budget, every GST council meeting, every new notification is a reason for a buyer to look for an explanation. A plain article on what the change means for exporters, or for startups, published in your name within a week, is knowledge sharing in the most literal sense. Do it for a year and you become the CA that a particular kind of business reads. [SEO for consultants](/blog/seo-for-consultants) covers how that writing gets found in search, and it applies to a CA practice without alteration.

**Personal branding for a CA is mostly this.** People search for "personal branding for CA" hoping for a trick. There isn't one. A partner who explains one useful thing a week on LinkedIn, in plain language, for the kind of client the firm serves, will be known within a year. A partner who posts congratulations on the firm's anniversary will not. Decide early whether the firm is known through one partner or as a firm, because it changes how you grow. [Founder brand vs company brand](/blog/founder-brand-vs-company-brand) is about exactly that fork.

**Speak in small rooms.** An industry association, a chamber of commerce chapter, an exporters' forum, a startup incubator. Thirty business owners with the problem you have chosen are worth more than three hundred CAs at a professional seminar, pleasant as those are.

**Build the website for the buyer who looks you up.** Most buyers who hear your name will look you up before they call. They should find who you are for, the problems you handle, how the first engagement works and what the partners have written. Not a list of the same nine services every firm lists.

**Work with the other advisors at the table.** Lawyers, company secretaries, investment advisors, bankers. Each sees your kind of client at a different moment. Being useful to them, by sharing what you know about the clients you have in common, puts your name in front of the buyer when the decision is being made. The law firm I mentioned found that the choice of advisor was usually made inside the client's circle of other advisors, and it was never in that room until it made a point of being.

None of this involves asking anyone for work. All of it makes you easy to choose.

## How to Get Your First Clients as a New CA Practice

![A new practice building its first ring of clients](/images/blog/get-clients-for-ca-firm/inline-4.webp)

A newly qualified CA starting a practice has a sharper version of the same problem: no inherited clients and no referral network yet. The usual advice is to take any work that comes. That fills the first year and empties the next five, because a practice built on whatever came becomes the family doctor without the family.

The better start is to pick the kind of client you understand best on day one. Perhaps you articled with a firm that served exporters, or you spent two years in a startup's finance team, or your family runs a business in a particular trade. That is your first position. Write about it, speak about it, and tell the people you already know, plainly, what you do and for whom. Telling people you know what you do is not soliciting work; it is answering a question they would ask anyway. Do the first engagements well and ask nothing of the client except permission to describe the kind of problem you solved. A practice that starts narrow grows by recommendation inside one community, which is far faster than waiting for the whole city to notice.

## A Marketing Routine That Survives Tax Season

The enemy of marketing in a CA firm is the calendar. Between the July returns, the September audits, and the year-end rush, there are long stretches where nobody in the practice has a free hour. So the routine has to be small, and it has to keep going in the busy months, because the enquiries it produces arrive a quarter later.

- **One useful article a month**, on a change in the law or a common problem, written for the kind of client you have chosen. In the busy months, a short note is enough.
- **One post a week** from the partner who is the firm's public face, drawn from a question a client asked that week.
- **A short note to five people a week**: past clients, other advisors, people you met in a room. Something useful in each, no request.
- **One small room a quarter**, chosen for who is in it.
- **One look at where new work came from, every month**, twenty minutes, so you know which of this is working.

That is two to three hours a week. For how it fits into a wider system for getting clients beyond referrals, the [guide to stopping your reliance on referrals](/blog/stop-relying-on-referrals) is where to start.

## Questions CAs Ask Me

### How do I get clients for my CA firm?

Choose a kind of client or a kind of problem, and become known for it. Package your advice into something with a name, a scope and an end, with a small first step in front of it. Then get found: write plainly about the problems your chosen clients face, speak where they gather, and build relationships with the other advisors who see them. Referrals still come, but now they come for a reason.

### Can a CA advertise or do digital marketing in India?

Chartered accountants are barred from soliciting clients or work, and advertising is allowed only within the limits set by the ICAI Council's guidelines, so check the current Code of Ethics before anything promotional. Sharing knowledge, speaking, publishing articles and having an informative website are generally open, and they are the parts of digital marketing for chartered accountants that work best anyway.

### What is a good marketing strategy for chartered accountants?

Being found rather than chasing. A clear position, an advisory offer beyond compliance, a steady stream of useful writing in the partners' names, and a presence in the rooms and circles where your chosen clients make decisions. Ads and cold outreach are mostly closed off by the rules, and they would win you price shoppers if they were not.

### How does a new CA get clients?

Start narrow, with the kind of client you already understand from articleship, a past job or your family's trade. Write and speak about that client's problems, tell the people you know plainly what you do, and do the first engagements well. A practice that is known in one community grows by recommendation inside it much faster than a general practice waiting to be noticed.

### Is personal branding worth it for a CA?

Yes, if it means sharing useful knowledge regularly in your own name. A partner who explains one practical thing a week, for one kind of client, becomes known in that community within a year. Decide whether the firm is known through a partner or as a firm, because that choice shapes how far it can grow.

## A CA Practice That Only Grows When a Client Mentions You?

The family doctor and the cardiologist work under the same rules. One waits for families to grow. The other is sought out by name. The difference is not skill and it is not permission to advertise. It is that one of them chose what to be known for, and then made sure the people who needed it could find her.
