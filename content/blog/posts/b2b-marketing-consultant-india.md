Search for a B2B marketing consultant in India and you will get a page of agencies. Top ten lists, full-funnel specialists, "India's top-rated" this and that, each one with a services menu longer than a wedding caterer's. Somewhere in that list are a few people who actually do consulting, and from the search results you cannot tell which ones.

That is a problem for you as a buyer, and I should declare my interest before going further: I am one of the people in that list. So treat this as a buyer's guide written by a seller, which is exactly the kind of guide you should read with one eyebrow raised. I will try to earn the other eyebrow by telling you when not to hire someone like me, and by giving you the questions that expose a vendor dressed up as an advisor. Including the ones you should ask me.

## What a Marketing Consultant Does That an Agency Does Not

The simplest way to see the difference is to ask what you are paying for.

An agency sells execution. You pay for posts published, campaigns run, pages built, and leads delivered, usually on a monthly retainer. A good agency is very good at this, and if you already know what to say, to whom, and through which channel, an agency is the right hire. The work is doing.

A consultant sells diagnosis and design. You pay for someone to work out why marketing is not producing the sales it should, and then to build the fix: the positioning, the message, the few channels worth your time, and the weekly rhythm that keeps them running. The work is deciding, and then making the decision stick inside your firm.

A doctor and a pharmacy is the analogy I use. The pharmacy is essential, and nobody wants a doctor who also insists on selling the medicine, because the prescription starts to look suspiciously like the stock. An agency asked "what should we do?" will, with the best intentions, recommend the things it sells. That is not dishonesty. It is the shape of the business model.

So a B2B marketing consultant for a services firm should leave you with three things an agency usually does not: a clear decision about who you are for and why they should pick you, a system your own people can run, and the ability to brief any agency well if you do hire one later. If the engagement ends and nothing works without the consultant, you bought an agency with a nicer title.

## The Problems Worth Hiring For: Positioning, Visibility, Lead Generation

![A sales symptom traced back to its three marketing causes](/images/blog/b2b-marketing-consultant-india/inline-1.webp)

Most firms that call me describe a sales problem. Enquiries are thin, proposals stall, prices get squeezed, the founder is the only one who can close. After twenty-odd years in marketing, and after building and selling firms of my own, my view is that the sales problem is usually a marketing problem. It shows up in sales because that is where the money is counted. It starts earlier, in one of three places.

**Positioning.** Prospects cannot tell you apart from the other five firms they are talking to, so they compare you on price. The fix is choosing who you serve and what you are the obvious choice for, which I have written about in [positioning for consulting firms](/blog/positioning-for-consulting-firms). This is the hardest of the three to do on your own, because you are too close to your own work to see what is actually different about it.

**Visibility.** The right buyers have never heard of you, so the only people who find you are the ones a friend sends. A good firm can stay invisible for fifteen years on referrals alone. The fix is a point of view published steadily in the places your buyers already look, which is cheaper than most firms fear; see [building brand awareness without an ad budget](/blog/b2b-brand-awareness-small-firms).

**Lead generation.** Even when people know you, nothing turns attention into conversations on a schedule. Marketing starts and stops with the founder's calendar. The fix is a small, repeatable system for lead generation, the subject of the [pillar guide on getting clients beyond referrals](/client-acquisition-system).

If your problem sits in one of these three, a consultant is worth talking to. If it sits somewhere else, keep reading, because the next section may save you the fee.

## When You Should Not Hire a Marketing Consultant

I turn down more conversations than people expect, and almost always for one of three reasons.

**You cannot deliver more work.** If your team is already stretched and a jump in enquiries would mean missed deadlines, marketing is not the constraint. Hiring and delivery are. Fix capacity first, or more enquiries will simply produce more unhappy clients, faster.

**The service itself is weak.** Marketing amplifies what is there. If clients leave unhappy, renewals are rare, and referrals have dried up because the work disappoints, then better positioning just introduces more people to the disappointment. I would rather tell you that in the first conversation than discover it in month three.

**The founder has no time to give.** This one is uncomfortable. A consultant can diagnose and design without much of your time, but the decisions are yours, and in a B2B services firm the founder's point of view is usually the raw material. If you cannot give two or three hours a week for a couple of months, the work will stall, and you will conclude that consultants do not work. The consultant was fine. The calendar was full.

There is a fourth, softer case. If you already know exactly what to do and only need hands to do it, hire the agency or a good marketing executive. Paying someone to tell you what you already know is an expensive way to feel reassured.

## Questions to Ask Before You Engage Anyone (Including Me)

![Five questions that sort advisors from vendors](/images/blog/b2b-marketing-consultant-india/inline-2.webp)

These questions are designed to sort advisors from vendors. A good consultant will be glad you asked. A vendor will get vague.

**"What would make you tell me not to hire you?"** An advisor has an answer, because they have turned down work before. A vendor will tell you everyone can benefit.

**"What will I own at the end?"** Listen for documents, decisions, and a routine your team runs. If the answer is "results" with no description of what is left behind, you are buying a retainer.

**"How will you diagnose before you prescribe?"** A consultant who proposes a LinkedIn programme and a website rebuild in the first meeting, before seeing your enquiry history or talking to a single client, is prescribing without an examination. My own first step is a [one-week diagnosis](/pipeline-reality-check) of where new business actually comes from, before anyone recommends anything.

**"Show me a past engagement like mine, and what changed."** Ask for the situation, the decision, and the outcome, not a logo wall. Expect some client names to be withheld; many B2B services firms do not want competitors knowing who advised them. But the story should be specific enough to believe. The [case studies on this site](/case-studies) are written that way, anonymised, with the work as it happened.

**"Who actually does the work?"** In a larger firm, the senior person sells and a junior team delivers. That may be fine, but you should know before you sign.

You will notice I have not included "what does it cost?". Ask it, of course. But cost is the wrong first filter, because a cheap engagement that recommends the wrong thing is the most expensive option on the list. More on cost below.

## What Buyers Ask Me Most Often

### How much does B2B marketing consulting cost in India?

It varies more than any other question here, so I will explain what drives it rather than quote a number. The three drivers are scope (a positioning decision alone versus a full system), duration (a few weeks versus several months), and how much of the implementation the consultant does rather than your team. Be wary of two ends of the market: the very cheap engagement that is really a template, and the long, open-ended retainer with no defined end point. Neither tells you what you will own when it finishes. A fair engagement has a scope, a timeline, and a list of things you keep.

### Which is the best B2B marketing agency or consultant in India?

There is no single answer, and anyone who claims the title in a search result has mostly shown that they are good at search. The best choice is the one that fits your problem. If you need execution at volume, look at agencies that specialise in your sector. If you need to work out what to execute and why, look for a consultant who diagnoses first. Then run the questions above on both.

### Should I hire a consultant or build an in-house marketing team?

Usually both, in order. A consultant is most useful before the first marketing hire, because the hardest decisions (who you serve, what you say, where you show up) should be made before someone is hired to carry them out. Hiring a marketing executive into a firm with no positioning is asking a junior person to invent the firm's strategy, which rarely ends well for either of you.

### How long before a marketing consultant shows results?

Positioning and messaging decisions can be made in weeks, and you often feel the effect in sales conversations quickly, because prospects stop asking how you differ from everyone else. Visibility and lead generation take longer, typically several months of steady publishing and outreach before enquiries change in a way you can measure. Anyone promising a full pipeline in thirty days is selling you ads.

## Wondering Whether a Consultant Is the Right Next Step?

Hiring a B2B marketing consultant in India, or anywhere, comes down to one honest question: is the thing stopping your growth a decision, or a lack of hands? If it is hands, hire an agency or a team. If it is a decision, about who you serve, what you say, and how you get in front of the right people every week, then an outside view is the fastest way to make it, and the cheapest way to avoid making it wrong.
